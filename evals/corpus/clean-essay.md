The best product review I ever sat through lasted eleven minutes.

The PM opened with the number that mattered: churn in the annual cohort had doubled since March. No agenda slide. No "context setting." She put the retention curve on screen, pointed at the elbow, and said the drop started the week we shipped the new billing page.

Then she showed us three support tickets. Real ones, with the customer names blurred. All three said some version of the same thing: I didn't know my card would be charged today. The billing page had buried the renewal date under a expandable section that our own analytics showed 4% of users opened.

Her proposed fix was small. Move the date above the fold, add a reminder email at day 23. Engineering estimated it at a week and a half. The CFO asked what it would cost us in failed-payment recovery revenue, which was a fair question, and she had the number ready: about $40k a quarter, against $310k in churn.

We approved it in the room.

The fix shipped twelve days later, and the cohort curve bent back within two billing cycles. Total cost: one designer-week, nine engineer-days, and one slightly annoyed CFO who came around when the Q3 numbers landed.

I think about that meeting whenever someone tells me they need another week to make the deck sing. The deck didn't sing. It had four slides and a screenshot of a support ticket. What it had instead was a person who knew exactly what was broken, what it cost, and what she wanted from the people in the room.
